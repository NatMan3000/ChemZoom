# ChemZoom - product

## Who it is for

Nathan (adult, technical, prints a lot on a Bambu Lab P2S) and his son Josh (12). They print with PLA, PETG, TPU, ABS and ASA and want to understand what those plastics actually are and why they behave differently. Both use it together and separately, on a desktop and on a phone (`spec/design-intent.md` P2, which holds Nathan's own answers on who it is for and how it should feel; where the two disagree, that file wins).

## The job

Make the invisible visible. Zoom from a spool of filament down to the atoms, see how the atoms bond into molecules and the molecules into chains, and connect that to things they have seen at the printer: PLA sagging in a hot car, ABS warping off the bed, TPU bending, ASA surviving the sun, PETG stringing when it is wet.

## Register

A science museum exhibit you can touch, not a textbook and not a kids' game. Pitched so a curious 12-year-old gets every line on the first read; a deeper layer with the real names and numbers for the adult, behind one app-wide simple/deeper switch (`spec/design-intent.md` P10). Australian English.

## Must be true

- Every fact shown has a source the app can show (PubChem, the maker's data sheet, an encyclopedia).
- Works offline once loaded (PWA), and on a phone as well as a desktop.
- Built to grow: filament is the first collection. Later collections (kitchen chemistry, why ice floats) are new data on the same viewers.
- Nothing moves on screen unless the user made it move, and it stops by itself.

## Anti-references

- Lab software (PyMOL, Avogadro): grey panels, jargon first.
- Kid apps: cartoon mascots, Comic Sans, bouncing everything.
- Wall-of-text encyclopedia pages.
